# StickLab v0.5.2 — Physics-powered walking

## Why

Earlier resident behavior nudged every ragdoll joint sideways. Figures slid or toppled, and player arrows had to remain pressed to sustain movement. They had no stride generation or grounded jump controller.

## New behavior

- A small dependency-free `src/locomotion.js` actuator drives physical joints using **Verlet velocity impulses**, without directly relocating bodies or bypassing collision handling.
- **Alternating leg steps:** left and right feet receive staggered horizontal and lift impulses while touching the floor or simple fixed platforms.
- **Balance and stopping:** the torso receives capped corrective impulses while grounded; releasing direction brakes horizontal movement gradually.
- **Ground-only jumps:** controlled vertical impulses act on hip, chest, knees and feet, with a simulation-tick cooldown. No midair jumps are added.
- **High-level Go Here:** use **Grab** to select a resident or ragdoll, press **Go Here** in the sidebar, then tap a point in the world. The figure is released to AI, travels under physics, and stops when it reaches the destination threshold. A small target marker shows the destination.
- **Automatic behavior:** existing Explore/Help/Avoid residents use the same gait controller. Manual left/right/jump continues to work for players who want direct input.
- **Save-compatible goals:** v4 scene saves accept finite destinations from 0 to the world width; old scenes normalize to null destinations and remain importable.
- **Performance:** foot and torso motors are constant work per active character; Eco/Standard/High AI caps, sparse AI decisions, and spatial collision candidate checks remain.

## How to use

1. Place a **Resident** or select an existing figure using **Grab**.
2. Open the menu and press **📍 Go Here**.
3. Tap the destination on the playfield and watch the figure attempt to walk there.
4. If you want it to roam instead, select it again and choose Explore and investigate or Rest. You can still control a figure with the on-screen direction keys.

This is inexpensive *game physics*, not biomechanical simulation. The character can lose balance, slip, fall off platforms, or be blocked by obstacles. Global path planning, full-body inverse kinematics, moving-platform prediction, and recovery from every fall remain follow-up work. This release does **not** make every object traversable or automatically solve every physics puzzle.

## Verification

Unit tests exercise alternating stance and swing legs, grounded versus airborne forces, one-shot jumping, platform support, arrival stop, and goal validation. The gameplay tests cover a tap-to-walk command, real physical travel and v4 roundtripping. Chrome touch regression tests check the Go Here button and drag-free world destination selection on a phone-size viewport. CI also ensures the hosted and standalone HTML embed the same controller.
